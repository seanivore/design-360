## Designing Fashion From Character

Most AI fashion imagery starts with a prompt and hopes for a good picture. I started somewhere else
entirely — with a person.

The premise: a collection should solve a real want, and real wants belong to people. So before any
image existed, there was a character, a story, and a personality that changed over time. The clothes
came from who that character was becoming.

### **The Personality Matrix**

The engine of this work was a matrix — a structured way to turn a character into wearable concepts.

IMAGE: `flow-narrative-ai-fashion-design-1.webp`
NEW MEDIA: a diagram of the matrix — one axis of archetypes/personality states, one of aesthetic
movements — resolving into outfit-concept groups. This is the centerpiece visual.

  + How the Matrix Worked
    - I wrote actual short stories — real narratives for each character
    - As a character's personality shifted through the story, the matrix mapped those shifts to
      outfit-concept groups and aesthetics built specifically for that moment in their arc
    - Archetypes defined genuine consumer needs and desires, so each look solved something real
    - The matrix drew on art history movements for archetypal grounding, and on astrology for
      personality definition *(cross-link out to B3 art history and B1 astro here)*

### **The Agentic Fashion Designer**

The matrix fed an agentic system that handled the creative pipeline end to end.

IMAGE: `flow-narrative-ai-fashion-design-2.webp`
NEW MEDIA: a before/after of prompt refinement — an early generic look beside the matrix-driven
result — to make the "perfecting the prompt" point visible.

  + From Story to Finished Look
    - Copy AI produced descriptive clips directly from the matrix — the bridge from narrative to
      prompt
    - Those clips drove image generation in Adobe Firefly, producing tens of options per concept
    - One was selected and refined further in Photoshop with generative fill
    - The agents ran concept generation through refinement autonomously, exploring multiple design
      directions at once and improving them through evaluation cycles
    - This is what "perfecting prompts" actually means here — not better wording, but a system that
      knew the character well enough to ask the right thing

## The Lookbooks

The matrix and the agents produced collections. Each one was published as its own kind of book.

### **A Shoppable Lookbook**

### **A Framer CMS Magazine**

SOURCE: YouTube `https://youtu.be/DvdaYVqqOY0`.

  + One Component, Eighty-One Spreads

Every collection here is wearable, on-trend, and genuinely designed — but the real deliverable isn't
the clothes. It's the method: start with a character worth dressing, and the collection designs
itself.
