## Social Strategy & Production

In 2011, I began my career in social media on a two-person team: my mentor [Helena Soh](https://www.linkedin.com/in/helenasoh/), and myself. Helena took the time to teach me copywriting excellence. I brought an expertise in visual storytelling to our efforts. 

Together, we left a lasting impression on the history of the organization, influenced the lifestyle choices of literal *billions* of people, all while pioneering strategies that later became industry standards. 

### Our First Viral Moments

Experimenting with new platform features and formats, we tapped into the frontier of social media marketing. Adding powerful taglines to visuals created topical narratives — and that hit even harder because no other brands were designing graphics on the fly.

#### The Same But Different 

In short order, Facebook added video sharing. Our tactics were the same: Be the first brand editing videos on the fly. Garnering multi-millions of impressions per post, our video work almost immediately started grabbing the attention of colleagues around the globe. 

The online growth was staggering and the success was quickly acknowledged in-house when the financial investment into our work increased tenfold. This was our opportunity to grow our team right alongside our audience, hiring assistants, strategists, and eventually an entirely new department that started out creating video exclusively for social media.

## Strategic Architect & Operational Lead

Helena and my hands had started to reach in every department of the global organization with a lofty task of shifting the entire organization's strategy from a traditional-communications model to a social-first model. Process had to be broken down and rebuilt, remaining assertive and confident through constant challenges and resistance. 

While leadership insulated the team from institutional friction, I owned the data, built the logic models that defended our strategy, and systematically trained global affiliates to build campaigns from the digital asset outward. 

Our end goal for the many departments that all focused on different, highly specialized content, was: Think social-first. Think visual-first. 

Holding that line took strategic assertiveness. We had to stop fielding urgent, last-minute requests for campaign input and instead get departments to tie us in from the start. The proof did the persuading: a global email sharing the press and earned coverage a properly-run campaign had earned almost always drew a reply-all from the president, in awe of the work. The message landed — use the process, and we all win.

This shift involved consulting on strategy and giving tutorials on creative production to the marketing teams of the organization's newly opened affiliate offices. I worked closely with employees in India, Australia, London, and Germany. 

In my original social marketing department my role was a bit removed and elevated: I was now routed on all digital content, where I quickly developed a reputation for offering lengthy, logical, highly-detailed feedback that transformed entire video cuts, optimizing them properly for social media — something our traditional production teams were less accustomed to, and part of our new organization-wide endeavor.
