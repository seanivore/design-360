## Social Strategy & Production

In 2011, I began my career in social media on a two-person team: my mentor [Helena Soh](https://www.linkedin.com/in/helenasoh/), and myself. Helena took the time to teach me copywriting excellence. I brought an expertise in visual storytelling to our efforts. 

Together, we left a lasting impression on the history of the organization, influenced the lifestyle choices of literal *billions* of people, and pioneered strategies that later became industry standards. 
  
### Our First Viral Moments

Those who work in marketing know that part of the job is learning and keeping up with the forever changing technology, and this was a rapidly evolving era for social media. It wasn't unheard of for companies to produce graphics with text and imagery, but they were working with long turnaround. 

Helena and I knew the nature of social media was its pace, that was part of what kept us so passionate, so when I brought my personal Mac in for the first time looking to edit some images *off* of the corporate Lenovo Thinkpads so that we could actually get some work done, it was fairly natural to start making images as quickly as one of us had an idea. It was then that we became the first brand on Facebook designing effective graphics on the fly.

    - Think 'visual-first'

    - Perfect a ~1 sentence post copy until punchy
    - Stay culturally relevant; this was easy because we were cool hipsters

    - The juxtaposition of the footwear and the abused animal hit hard
    - Users were angry and sad, which creates sharing and engagement
    - UGGs were peak popularity, so it felt so relatable

    - This success taught me the term "earned media"

    - Users didn't hesitate to engage when emotional

#### The Same But Different 

It wasn't long before Facebook advanced from photo sharing to video sharing. Our tactics were the exact same: Be the first brand to edit videos on the fly, creating and posting three or more a day.

Video was a breakout moment, garnering massive reach. Our bar, or goal, for a solid post quickly was in the millions of impressions per post. This grabbed the attention of colleagues around the globe.

We were officially recognized internally for our consistent, staggeringly successful audience growth when our yearly budget was increased tenfold. This was used to grow our team along with our audience. We upgraded our hardware and started hiring assistants, strategists, and in time, an entire video department specifically for social media videos.

### Leveraging Engagement To Influence Culture
It didn't take long to come up with a strategy to flex our newly established audience-base muscles. It was clear we had enough of an audience to generate media attention levels of engagement. Now the strategy could be pushed deeper, potentially dictating the terms of anyone's conversation.

## Strategic Architect & Operational Lead

Helena's and my hands had started to reach in every department of the global organization with a lofty task of shifting the entire organization's strategy from a traditional-communications model to a social-first model. Process had to be broken down and rebuilt, and we needed to remain assertive and confident through constant challenges and resistance. 

While leadership insulated the team from institutional friction, I owned the data, built the logic models that defended our strategy, and systematically trained global affiliates to build campaigns from the digital asset outward. 

Our end goal for the many departments that all focused on different, highly specialized content, was: Think social-first. Think visual-first.
