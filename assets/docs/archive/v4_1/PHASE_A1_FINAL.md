## Social Strategy & Production

In 2011, I began my career in social media on a two-person team: my mentor [Helena Soh](https://www.linkedin.com/in/helenasoh/), and myself. Helena took the time to teach me copywriting excellence. I brought an expertise in visual storytelling to our efforts. 

Together, we left a lasting impression on the history of the organization, influenced the lifestyle choices of literal *billions* of people, all while pioneering strategies that later became industry standards. 

### Our First Viral Moments

Experimenting with new platform features and formats, we tapped into the frontier of social media marketing. Adding powerful taglines to visuals that created topical narratives hit even harder because no brands were designing graphics on the fly.

#### Viral [UGGs Image in Tweet](https://x.com/peta/status/396670298465914880?s=20) Published in YahooNews

It didn't take long before we had gotten a reputation for crashing the GLOBAL organization's robust website from sending hundreds of thousands of visits in just minutes. 

In these early years both Facebook and BuzzFeed were requesting meetings with us to discuss our new social media strategy.

**BULLETED LIST WITH INDENTED SUB-LISTS**
  + Why live coverage:
    - Helena had come to the organization with robust experience covering Hollywood red carpet events. 
    - I thrived in the fast paced environment, spit-balling ideas, and pulling from my powerful visual-focused memory of exactly what images we had as resources. 
    - It was an opportunity to insert our messages into real-time, national conversations; a rare opportunity to speak to the entire country at once.

  + Our process: 
    - Sitting together in the office with a large TV, we carefully watched for opportunities that could be tied back to our messaging priorities.
    - Helena would constantly draft possible phasing and blurbs that might evolve into tweets or captions.
    - I stayed close, paging through my mental and digital archives of images, exploring how to translate her concepts into visuals.

  + A spotted opportunity: 
    - Helena spotted Joe Namath walking out onto the field in a ridiculous fur coat. 
    - This instantly mentally flagged our emotional photojournalist work investigating fur farms across the globe.
    - After rapidly paging through those images, only pausing for one that "gut checked" us in the split second it was on the screen, we paired the visual with copywriting crafted to amplify the emotional gut check. 

  + The execution:
    - The resulting post had a technically beautiful photograph of an animal on a farm, that had a haunting feel we tapped into by adding the words "NOT YOURS TO WEAR"
    - The tagline doubled as a direct statement AT Joe Namath; this is what we called "sharability" because posting to your own feed represented in users a statement of their own feelings or identity. 
    - Viral-status was all but guaranteed thanks to our corespondents tapping in celebrities asking them to kick off the re-sharing.
    - The work paid off when online articles like 'Brand Dot Com' picked it up and published it in their own articles.

#### The Same But Different 

In sort order Facebook added video sharing. Our tactics were the same: Be the first brand editing videos on the fly. Garnering multi-millions of impressions per post, our video work almost immediately started grabbing the attention of colleagues around the globe. 

The online growth was staggering and the success was quickly acknowledged in-house when the financial investment into our work increased tenfold. This was our opportunity to grow our team right along side of our audience, hiring assistants, strategists, and eventually an entirely new department that started out creating video exclusively for social media.

In little more than a month we were ready to flex this newly established audience-base. We knew from the start of planning a campaign that we could access enough engagement to capture media outlet attention, allowing us to adjust our strategic focus in a powerful way.
